# Writing examples

[Русский](texts.md) | English

All briefs below are fictional teaching examples, not reports about real events or the author's biography. English is explicitly requested in these briefs; the skill's default remains Russian.

## From a vague opening to a specific one

Every detail below comes from a fictional brief. These are possible editing directions, not required phrases or claims about the author's actual experience.

| Vague opening | Possible opening | What should follow |
|---|---|---|
| A few thoughts on Python settings | The string "false" becomes True when passed directly to bool(). | Explain that a nonempty string is truthy under this particular conversion. Suggest explicitly recognizing true and false and rejecting unknown values. Do not generalize to all DEBUG settings or frameworks |
| AI helps fix bugs | In this teaching example, the agent fixed one input. Another still failed. | Keep the fictional status clear. Show an additional check without promising proof of program correctness |
| On consumables in games | The final boss is defeated. All 47 potions made it to the credits too. | The recognizable scene is enough. Do not claim the author played it or add advice and a closing question |
| What do you think of this approach? | An unknown flag value: stop startup or disable the feature? | Before the closing question, explain the consequences: stopping exposes a configuration error; continuing with the feature off may hide it. Ask which conditions would change the choice |
| About the timeline | Design planned for November 12: please confirm | Keep the planned status, approval-delay condition and request to confirm the plan in the email body. Do not invent urgency |

A specific opening need not reveal the resolution. A teaching example could begin: "One test input passes; another fails. Let's examine why the fix worked for only one of them." The subject is clear while the explanation remains in the piece, which must provide it.

A strong original such as "Local notes won't move to your new phone on their own" can stay when the rest explains the transfer. A reply to "Thanks, that helped!" can be just "Glad it helped!" When asked to fix typos only, even a vague opening stays intact.

## Photo caption

**Brief:** a photo of an empty beach after rain. One short, calm English caption, without an invented trip story.

> An empty beach after the rain.

The caption uses the supplied scene without inventing feelings, weather history or who took the photo.

## Brand announcement

**Brief:** the fictional List workshop starts taking notebook orders on November 12. Prices from 900 RUB. Production usually takes five business days. Orders by direct message. Warm and calm, in English.

> From November 12, you can order a notebook from List.
>
> Prices start at 900 RUB. Making one usually takes five business days.
>
> Send us a direct message to order.

The starting price, business days and "usually" remain. No stock levels, paper quality or limited availability are invented.

## Useful post with an offer

**Brief:** the fictional List workshop helps beginners choose notebook ruling and invites orders. Blank pages leave room for drawing; dots help align lines. Orders open November 12, from 900 RUB, production usually takes five business days, orders by direct message. Write in English.

> Choose your first notebook's ruling around what you want to do on the page.
>
> For drawing without guides, blank pages may suit you. If you want help keeping lines aligned, consider dots.
>
> From November 12, you can order a notebook from List. Prices start at 900 RUB; production usually takes five business days. Send us a direct message to order.

The explanation helps even without a purchase. The offer follows the subject and retains its terms. This structure serves a sales brief; it is not added to an ordinary explanation.

## A poll with a clear choice

**Brief:** choose the workshop's next beginner tutorial topic: paper choice, stitching or attaching the cover, with room for another suggestion. English plain text; answers in comments. No promised date or participation gift.

> What should the next post about making your first notebook explain?
>
> A. Choosing paper.
> B. Stitching pages.
> C. Attaching the cover.
> D. Another topic: suggest your own.
>
> Leave a letter or your topic in the comments.

One question serves a specific decision. Options are distinct, the response method is clear, and readers can suggest a topic outside the list.

## Edit precisely

**Original:** "We are pleased to announce that we plan to open registration on November 12. Participation may cost around 3,000 RUB. The final price has not yet been approved."

> We plan to open registration on November 12. Participation may cost around 3,000 RUB, but the final price has not been approved yet.

A plan remains a plan. An approximate, unapproved price does not become a firm quote.

## Voice from a sample

**Fictional sample:** "Wrote the to-do list. Read the to-do list. Enough paperwork for today."

**New brief:** the author says they turned off notifications to read a book. Concise, lightly ironic English.

> Notifications off. Book open. At least my phone thinks I'm busy.

Rhythm and humor transfer; the new action comes from the new brief.

## Missing material

**Request:** "Find something fresh and write me a post." No topic or destination is known.

> What subject should I search: technology, culture or your professional field? You can name another topic.

A random story cannot stand in for the author's interests. Clarify the topic first; the destination can follow if it affects the result. If the conversation already supplies their interests, do not ask again.

## Search unavailable

**Request:** fresh photography news, but neither search nor a browser is available.

> I can't check fresh news in this environment. Give me a source link or its text, and I can draft from it. I can also write a photography post without a news premise.

The agent does not substitute invented news. Date and conflicting-source scenarios are in the [evaluation guide](../evals/README_en.md).

## One platform, different deliverables

**Brief:** an English YouTube description. The List workshop video shows choosing paper, stitching pages and attaching a notebook cover. For beginners. No timestamps or extra links.

> Make a notebook from scratch with the List workshop. We show you how to choose paper, stitch the pages and attach the cover. For anyone making their first notebook who wants to understand the process.

**Same material, different task:** an introduction the author will read on camera. Spoken words only, in English.

> Want to make a notebook but aren't sure where to start? In this video, we'll go through choosing paper, stitching the pages and attaching the cover. Let's start with the paper.

The second request already establishes speech. It does not need another format question. "For YouTube about notebooks" alone would warrant one question with choices.

## Business email

**Brief:** an English email to partner Anna. The team plans to send the design on November 12. If approval takes longer, it will notify her separately of a revised date. Ask whether the plan works.

> Hi Anna,
>
> We plan to send the design on November 12. If approval takes longer, we'll let you know separately about a revised date.
>
> Does that plan work for you?

The plan and condition remain. No invented signature, agreement or friendly backstory is added.

## Required fields and a reply

**Brief:** an English discussion post for a fictional notebook community. A title and body are required. No advertising. Compare blank and dotted paper without claiming personal use.

**Title:** Blank or dotted paper for a first notebook?

**Body:**

> Blank paper leaves the page open for sketches and layouts. Dots give you a guide for lines without a full grid. Which would you choose for a first notebook, and what would you use it for?

The title belongs to the requested post. The question serves a discussion brief rather than a universal engagement template.

**Different brief:** reply to "Can I order now?" using supplied facts: orders open November 12. Return only the English reply.

> Orders open on November 12. You'll be able to send us a direct message then.

The reply answers the question without restarting the announcement or adding a title.
